# Building an SME Digital Banking Ecosystem

> A product leadership case study on building and scaling an SME-focused digital banking platform in a highly regulated fintech environment.

---

## Overview

Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs) represent one of the largest business segments in Indonesia, yet many remain underserved by traditional banking solutions.

This project aimed to build a digital banking ecosystem that would help SMEs manage their financial activities through a unified platform, including payments, transfers, onboarding, and operational financial services.

Beyond launching new capabilities, the challenge was to create a scalable solution that balanced customer experience, regulatory compliance, fraud prevention, operational efficiency, and business growth.

This remains one of the most meaningful projects in my career—not only because of the business impact it generated, but also because of the incredible people and cultures involved in building it.

---

# My Role

**Product Lead / Product Leader**

Responsibilities included:

* Defining product vision and strategy
* Building and managing product roadmaps
* Leading Product Managers and Designers
* Driving cross-functional alignment across Engineering, Compliance, Operations, Finance, Legal, Marketing, and Business teams
* Managing strategic partnerships and vendors
* Supporting regulatory discussions and compliance initiatives
* Ensuring successful delivery and scalability of the platform

---

# Business Context

The SME market presents a unique challenge.

Unlike consumer products, SMEs vary significantly in terms of:

* Business size
* Industry type
* Operational maturity
* Financial behavior
* Transaction patterns

A small online seller, a restaurant owner, a distributor, and a manufacturing business may all be classified as SMEs, yet their needs can be completely different.

The goal was not to build every possible solution for every segment.

The goal was to identify the common needs shared by the majority of SMEs and build a scalable ecosystem around those needs.

---

# Key Challenges

## 1. Regulatory Ambiguity

One of the biggest challenges involved products with evolving regulatory interpretations, particularly Virtual Card capabilities.

The regulatory landscape was continuously evolving, requiring close collaboration with:

* Compliance teams
* Legal teams
* Banking partners
* Payment partners
* Regulators

Building quickly was important, but building compliantly was non-negotiable.

---

## 2. SME Segmentation Complexity

The SME segment is often treated as a single market.

In reality, it consists of hundreds of business profiles with different operational needs.

The challenge was determining:

* Which customer problems to prioritize
* Which capabilities should become platform standards
* Which requests should not be built despite customer demand

A product designed for everyone often ends up serving no one particularly well.

---

## 3. Competitive Market Landscape

During development, many large banks and financial institutions launched their own SME digital banking initiatives.

Competing against established organizations with larger resources required:

* Strong positioning
* Clear prioritization
* Fast execution
* Focused differentiation

---

## 4. Partner & Vendor Dependencies

The platform relied on multiple strategic partners and service providers.

Delivering a seamless customer experience required alignment across:

* Banking partners
* Payment providers
* Infrastructure vendors
* Mastercard
* Internal operational teams

Managing dependencies across organizations became a critical part of successful execution.

---

## 5. Fraud Prevention & Business Verification

Perhaps the most difficult challenge involved business verification.

Know Your Business (KYB) is significantly more complex than standard consumer KYC.

Common challenges included:

* Incomplete documentation
* Business legitimacy validation
* Risk assessment
* Fraud detection
* Regulatory compliance requirements

Balancing onboarding conversion and risk management required continuous iteration.

---

# Product Strategy

## Market Research & Positioning

Before defining the roadmap, extensive research was conducted to understand:

* SME pain points
* Existing market solutions
* Competitive positioning
* Adoption barriers
* Customer priorities

The objective was not to build the largest feature set, but to build the most relevant solution for the target market.

---

## Regulatory-First Product Development

Compliance was integrated into the product development process from the beginning.

Instead of treating compliance as a final approval step, regulatory considerations became part of:

* Discovery
* Design
* Development
* Release planning

This reduced rework and improved confidence in product decisions.

---

## Prioritizing the Majority

A common trap when building SME products is over-customization.

Many customer requests are valid but only solve problems for a small segment.

The strategy focused on identifying:

* Shared customer needs
* High-frequency operational problems
* Scalable platform capabilities

This allowed the team to maximize impact while maintaining execution efficiency.

---

## Continuous KYB Optimization

The onboarding experience went through multiple iterations.

Each iteration was driven by:

* Operational feedback
* User behavior analysis
* Fraud cases
* Conversion metrics
* Compliance findings

The objective was simple:

Improve onboarding efficiency without compromising risk controls.

---

## Building an In-House TRMS

To strengthen fraud prevention capabilities, we developed an internal:

**Transaction Risk Monitoring System (TRMS)**

The system enabled:

* Transaction monitoring
* Risk pattern identification
* Fraud detection
* Investigation support
* Risk mitigation workflows

The result was stronger fraud controls while maintaining a smooth customer experience.

---

# Strategic Partnership with Mastercard

Partnerships played an important role in the platform's development.

Working with Mastercard provided opportunities to explore payment innovations and strengthen the overall value proposition for SME customers.

The collaboration required balancing:

* Business objectives
* Technical integration
* Customer experience
* Compliance requirements
* Operational readiness

Partnership management became an important component of successful product delivery.

# Cross-Cultural Product Leadership

One of the most rewarding aspects of this journey was the team.

More than 90% of the engineering organization was based in Vietnam.

Leadership and stakeholders were distributed across:

* Indonesia
* Vietnam
* India
* Brazil

Building products in this environment required more than product expertise.

It required:

* Cross-cultural communication
* Empathy
* Trust-building
* Alignment across different working styles
* Shared accountability

What could have been a challenge ultimately became one of the project's greatest strengths.

The diversity of experiences and perspectives consistently helped us build better products and make better decisions.

To this day, it remains one of the most enjoyable and memorable teams I have had the privilege to work with.

* Improved onboarding efficiency through multiple KYB optimizations
* Strengthened fraud controls through TRMS implementation
* Increased operational scalability for future growth

### Organizational Impact

* Successfully coordinated multiple internal and external stakeholders
* Strengthened collaboration between product, engineering, compliance, operations, and business teams
* Built a strong culture of ownership across distributed teams

---

# Key Learnings

Building products for SMEs is not primarily a technology challenge.

It is a challenge of understanding people, balancing competing priorities, navigating uncertainty, and making disciplined product decisions.

This experience reinforced several beliefs:

* Not every customer request should become a feature.
* Compliance and product should work together, not against each other.
* Great partnerships accelerate growth.
* Diverse teams create better products.
* Product leadership is ultimately about helping people move in the same direction despite complexity.

And perhaps most importantly:

**The best products are not built by individuals. They are built by teams that trust each other, challenge each other, and share a common purpose.**
